Website RPM Guide

What Is a Good Website RPM?

Learn what a good website RPM can look like, why RPM varies between websites and which factors can influence revenue per 1,000 pageviews.

What is website RPM?

Website RPM stands for revenue per 1,000 pageviews. It is a useful metric for estimating how much revenue a website generates from its traffic.

RPM can be calculated by dividing total revenue by total pageviews and multiplying the result by 1,000.

RPM = Revenue ÷ Pageviews × 1,000

For example, if a website generates $120 from 10,000 pageviews, its RPM is $12.

So, what is a good RPM?

There is no single RPM number that is considered good for every website. A reasonable RPM depends on the website's niche, audience, traffic sources, geography, advertising setup and the type of content being published.

An RPM that looks low for one website may be perfectly reasonable for another. The most useful comparison is often against your own historical performance.

Why RPM varies between websites

1. Website niche

Different industries attract different levels of advertiser demand. Topics connected to valuable commercial decisions can sometimes attract stronger advertising demand than topics with less commercial intent.

2. Audience location

Visitor geography can have a significant effect on advertising revenue. Advertisers may place different values on audiences in different countries and regions.

3. Traffic source

Visitors arriving from search engines, social media, direct traffic and other sources can behave differently. Differences in engagement and user intent can influence monetization performance.

4. Pageviews per visitor

A visitor who views several pages creates more opportunities for advertising impressions than a visitor who views only one page.

5. Ad placement and user experience

The position and number of advertisements can affect both revenue and user experience. More advertising does not automatically mean better results.

How to improve website RPM

Improving RPM usually means increasing the amount of revenue generated from existing pageviews rather than simply trying to attract more visitors.

  • Improve content quality and relevance.
  • Focus on topics with stronger commercial intent.
  • Improve page speed and user experience.
  • Test advertising placements carefully.
  • Increase pages per visitor with useful internal links.
  • Analyze which traffic sources generate the most value.

Should you focus on RPM or traffic?

Both matter, but the best strategy is usually to improve them together.

Increasing traffic can create more pageviews and revenue, while improving RPM can increase the value of the traffic you already have.

For example, a website that increases monthly pageviews while also improving RPM can potentially grow revenue faster than a website that focuses only on traffic.

Track RPM over time

Instead of looking for one perfect RPM benchmark, track how your own RPM changes over time.

Look for changes after publishing new types of content, changing traffic sources, improving page layouts or testing different monetization strategies.

Consistent improvement is often more useful than trying to match an arbitrary industry number.

Final thoughts

A good website RPM is one that makes sense for your audience, niche and monetization model. There is no universal number that applies to every website.

The most important step is understanding your current RPM, tracking it consistently and looking for practical ways to increase the revenue generated by each 1,000 pageviews.
