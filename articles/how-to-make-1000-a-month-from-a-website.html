How to Make $1,000 a Month From a Website

The goal of $1,000 a month becomes much easier to plan when you break it into traffic, monetization rate and revenue per visitor instead of treating it as a single magic traffic number.

Start with the revenue target

$1,000 per month is $12,000 per year. The important question is not simply “How many visitors do I need?” but “How much revenue can each visitor or pageview reasonably produce?”

Path 1: Advertising

With display advertising, the basic planning formula is pageviews divided by 1,000 multiplied by RPM.

Required pageviews = $1,000 ÷ RPM × 1,000

At a $10 RPM, the simple model requires about 100,000 monthly pageviews. At $20 RPM, it requires about 50,000. Actual results vary, so treat these as scenarios rather than promises.

Path 2: Affiliate marketing

Affiliate revenue can reach a $1,000 target with fewer visitors when the audience has strong purchase intent and commissions are meaningful.

Affiliate revenue = clicks × conversion rate × average commission

For example, 1,000 affiliate clicks × 4% conversion × $25 commission = $1,000 gross expected commission. Getting those clicks is the challenge, which is why commercial content and strong search intent matter.

Path 3: Combine revenue streams

You do not need one source to generate the entire $1,000. A diversified model can be easier to build and less dependent on one platform.

  • $400 from advertising
  • $400 from affiliate commissions
  • $200 from a product, service or leads

The exact mix should follow what your audience actually wants. Adding monetization that harms user experience can reduce long-term value.

Focus on commercial intent

Not all traffic is equally valuable. A visitor searching for a product comparison, software alternative, buying guide or specific solution can have much stronger monetization potential than a visitor reading a general-interest article.

This makes keyword selection important. Build informational content to attract visitors, but connect relevant commercial pages and tools so that high-intent users have a useful next step.

A practical zero-budget plan

  1. Choose one narrow topic where useful search demand exists.
  2. Create genuinely helpful articles that answer specific questions.
  3. Build calculators or other free tools around recurring user problems.
  4. Link articles to the most relevant calculator and related guides.
  5. Test affiliate offers only where they genuinely help the reader.
  6. Track which pages attract traffic and which pages create commercial actions.
  7. Improve existing winners before endlessly publishing more pages.

What to avoid

  • Buying traffic before you have a conversion path.
  • Stuffing pages with unrelated affiliate links.
  • Assuming a single RPM benchmark applies to every niche.
  • Publishing dozens of thin AI-generated pages with little original value.
  • Spending money on tools before the site has proven demand.

FAQ

How many visitors do I need to make $1,000?

There is no fixed number. Advertising-only models may require substantial pageviews, while affiliate or high-intent models can reach the target with fewer visitors.

Can a new website make $1,000 quickly?

It can happen, but there is no reliable shortcut. Search traffic often takes time to develop, and affiliate earnings depend on actual conversions.

Should I focus on ads or affiliates?

For a small site, test both where appropriate. Advertising is simple but often needs volume; affiliates can be more valuable when visitors have strong purchase intent.

Key takeaway: Treat $1,000/month as a system of smaller targets: traffic, pageviews, RPM, clicks, conversions and commission. That makes the goal measurable and gives you several ways to improve.
