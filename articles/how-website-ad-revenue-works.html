How Website Ad Revenue Works

Website advertising can turn pageviews into revenue. But how does the money actually work? This guide explains impressions, clicks, CPM, CPC and RPM in simple terms.

How does website advertising work?

When a website displays advertising, advertisers pay to reach an audience. An advertising network can act as the connection between advertisers and publishers.

The website owner earns revenue based on the advertising model being used and the performance of the ads.

Depending on the network and campaign, revenue can be influenced by impressions, clicks, conversions and other factors.

What is an ad impression?

An impression generally represents an instance where an advertisement is displayed to a user.

If a page displays several advertisements, a single pageview can potentially generate multiple ad impressions.

However, an impression does not necessarily mean that somebody clicked the advertisement.

What is CPM?

CPM stands for cost per thousand impressions.

The "M" represents one thousand.

For example, a $5 CPM means an advertiser is paying approximately $5 for 1,000 qualifying ad impressions under that pricing model.

Publisher revenue can differ from the advertiser's gross cost because advertising platforms can have different pricing, fees and revenue-sharing arrangements.

What is CPC?

CPC means cost per click.

In a CPC-based advertising model, an advertiser pays when a qualifying user clicks an advertisement.

This means that the number of clicks and the value of those clicks can influence revenue.

What is RPM?

RPM means revenue per 1,000 pageviews.

RPM = (Revenue ÷ Pageviews) × 1,000

For example, if a website generates $50 from 5,000 pageviews:

($50 ÷ 5,000) × 1,000 = $10 RPM

RPM is useful because it gives a simple way to compare monetization performance across different traffic levels.

CPM vs CPC vs RPM

Metric Meaning What it measures
CPM Cost per 1,000 impressions Advertising cost based on impressions
CPC Cost per click Advertising cost based on clicks
RPM Revenue per 1,000 pageviews Publisher revenue efficiency

What affects website ad revenue?

Two websites with the same number of visitors can earn very different amounts from advertising.

Important factors can include:

  • Audience location
  • Website niche
  • Advertiser demand
  • Traffic source
  • Device type
  • Number of pageviews
  • Ad placement
  • Seasonality
  • User engagement

Why traffic alone does not determine revenue

It is tempting to think that more visitors automatically means proportionally more revenue.

In reality, traffic quality matters.

A website may attract a large amount of low-value traffic while another website attracts a smaller audience with stronger commercial intent.

The second website may potentially monetize its audience more efficiently.

Pageviews matter too

Consider two websites that each receive 10,000 visitors.

Website A averages one pageview per visitor.

10,000 visitors × 1 page = 10,000 pageviews

Website B averages two pageviews per visitor.

10,000 visitors × 2 pages = 20,000 pageviews

If other conditions were similar, Website B would have twice as many pageviews available for monetization.

Example: estimating advertising revenue

Suppose a website receives 50,000 pageviews per month and its effective RPM is $10.

(50,000 ÷ 1,000) × $10 = $500

The estimated advertising revenue would therefore be $500 for that scenario.

This is an example calculation, not a guarantee of actual advertising earnings.

How can you increase ad revenue?

Website owners can work on both sides of the equation: increasing useful traffic and improving monetization efficiency.

  • Publish useful content that attracts relevant visitors.
  • Improve internal linking and user navigation.
  • Improve page speed and mobile usability.
  • Test advertising layouts carefully.
  • Monitor RPM and other revenue metrics.
  • Experiment with additional monetization methods.

Advertising is only one revenue stream

A website does not have to depend entirely on advertising.

Depending on the audience, other revenue opportunities can include affiliate marketing, digital products, sponsorships, memberships and services.

Combining relevant revenue streams can make a website's business model more diversified.

Final takeaway

Website ad revenue is influenced by impressions, clicks, audience quality, pageviews, advertiser demand and many other factors.

CPM and CPC help describe advertising economics, while RPM provides a useful publisher-focused view of revenue per 1,000 pageviews.

Understanding these metrics gives you a better foundation for deciding how much traffic your website needs and how to improve its monetization.

Key takeaway: Website traffic creates opportunities, but monetization efficiency determines how much value you can generate from that traffic. Track both.
