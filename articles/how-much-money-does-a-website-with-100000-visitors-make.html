How Much Does a Website With 100,000 Visitors Make?

100,000 monthly visitors is enough to make monetization strategy a serious business decision. But traffic still does not translate directly into income: pageviews, RPM, purchase intent and conversion rate determine the result.

Example: 100,000 visitors producing 150,000 pageviews at a $20 RPM would generate about $3,000 per month in advertising revenue. At $30 RPM, the same traffic would generate about $4,500.

Advertising Revenue

Use pageviews rather than visitors when calculating page RPM. A site with 100,000 visitors can have very different pageview totals depending on how many pages each visitor reads.

Affiliate Revenue

Imagine 100,000 visitors generate 10,000 affiliate clicks. At a 2% conversion rate there would be 200 conversions. With a $30 average commission, estimated gross commission would be $6,000 per month.

That scenario illustrates why commercial intent can be more important than traffic volume. A smaller number of highly qualified visitors can be more valuable than a much larger general audience.

What a 100,000-Visitor Site Should Optimize

  • Identify the pages with the strongest commercial intent.
  • Improve conversion rates before adding more offers.
  • Test advertising without damaging user experience.
  • Create comparison, buyer and problem-solving content.
  • Track revenue per visitor and revenue per page, not only traffic.

Bottom Line

There is no fixed amount that a 100,000-visitor website makes. A useful target is to build multiple monetization paths and measure which one produces the best revenue per qualified visitor.
