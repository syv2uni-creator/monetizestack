The zero-budget rule

If your budget is €0, do not start by buying services. Build the first version yourself, learn enough to recognize quality and identify the exact bottleneck. Once the site has traffic or revenue, reinvest only when a small project has a clear expected benefit.

1. Define the problem.Example: “I have impressions but weak click-through” is more useful than “I need SEO.”
2. Create a small deliverable.Prefer an audit, a landing page, a fixed number of articles or a specific technical fix over an open-ended retainer.
3. Compare providers.Look for relevant portfolios, clear scope, realistic timelines and useful buyer feedback.
4. Protect access.Use collaborator or limited-access accounts where possible. Avoid sharing passwords unnecessarily.
5. Measure the result.Keep investing only when the work creates useful assets or measurable progress.

What to avoid

  • Guaranteed Google rankings or guaranteed revenue.
  • Huge packages you cannot evaluate.
  • Monthly retainers before you understand the bottleneck.
  • Providers who cannot explain exactly what they will deliver.
  • Sharing your main passwords when safer access exists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy Fiverr services when my budget is zero?

No. First build and validate the site yourself. Outsourcing becomes more sensible after you have a proven bottleneck and a clear expected return.

Which service should I buy first?

There is no universal answer. Buy the smallest project that directly addresses your current bottleneck, whether that is a technical fix, a landing page, content or an SEO audit.

Does Fiverr guarantee freelancer quality?

No. Fiverr is a marketplace with independent sellers. Evaluate each provider, scope and deliverable yourself.
